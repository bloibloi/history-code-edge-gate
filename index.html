HISTORY

Breaking the Code at Bletchley Park

During the Second World War, the German military encrypted its radio traffic with the Enigma machine, an electromechanical device whose rotating wheels produced a cipher that changed with every keystroke. Polish mathematicians made the first breaks into Enigma in the 1930s and shared their techniques with Britain and France shortly before the war began.

At a country estate north of London called Bletchley Park, a team that included the mathematician Alan Turing built on that early work. Turing helped design an electromechanical machine called the Bombe, which sped through the possible Enigma rotor settings far faster than any human codebreaker could by hand. Intelligence recovered this way was classified under the codename Ultra and is credited by historians with shortening the war.
